How the program runs

Reps, scored honestly, repeated.

01

Mock interviews

Technical screens, behavioral rounds and full mocks. A session doesn't have to cover everything. A technical screen scores only the technical competencies it actually tested.

02

Coach scoring

A coach logs a 1–10 on every competency the mock covered, plus written notes. Every coach is a current UT student who transferred from UTSA. Andrew and Ben rate today.

03

Weighted profile

Each new mock counts twice as much as the one before it. Your profile tracks current ability rather than averaging away recent progress.

Why recency-weighted? A flat average punishes you for a bad first mock forever. Doubling the weight of each newer session means a member who was a 5 in September and a 9 in November reads as close to a 9, which is the number that actually predicts how the real interview goes.

The rating model

What gets scored.

Overall is a 1–10 composite of three pillars. Only competencies that have actually been tested count toward a score.

Reading the numbers

How a score is built

Each competency is scored 1 to 10 only on the mocks that actually tested it. Within a competency, mocks are weighted by recency: the newest counts times the one before it, 4 times the one before that, and so on. Pillar and overall scores renormalise over whatever has been tested, so an untested competency never drags a score down. It simply doesn't count yet.
