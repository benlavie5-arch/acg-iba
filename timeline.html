Recruiting Timeline

Two calendars running at once: the academic path from UTSA into UT, and the banking cycle that does not wait for it. This is the program's own schedule, month by month.

Track A

Academic path.

Getting into UT is the gating item. Nothing in Track B waits for it.

Verify the official dates yourself. The program calendar below is ours. UT's transfer and CAP deadlines are UT's, they move, and missing one cannot be undone. Confirm them on UT's admissions site.
August to December · freshman fall

Join the club, at UTSA

The joining window is your freshman fall. Joining later costs you the runway the whole plan depends on.

Freshman year · throughout

Build the transfer case

GPA and transferable credit hours are the whole argument. Confirm which UTSA courses count before you register, with advisors at both schools. CAP students: the credit-hour requirement is a hard condition, not a target.

Spring · deadline

Transfer application in

Check the current deadline and required materials on UT's own admissions site. This is the one date with no flexibility.

June

UT decision

The admission decision lands. Track B does not pause while you wait for it.

Sophomore year · at UT

24 credit hours, 12 each semester

A deliberately light load, split evenly across fall and spring. Sophomore year is when recruiting actually happens, and a heavy schedule is the most common reason members miss first rounds. Protect the calendar.

Track B

Banking recruiting.

Summer analyst recruiting runs roughly eighteen months ahead of the internship. Assume you are behind and work accordingly.

Aug to Nov · freshman year

Join the club

Baseline mock, first ratings, and a plan for the year.

Dec to Feb · freshman year

Technical training

The first real push. Accounting first, then valuation fundamentals. Target: a technical score around 5 by the end of February. That is deliberately not a high bar. It means the foundation is in and you can be built on.

March to April · freshman year

Start networking

Reach out to current seniors and recent alumni. Short conversations, low stakes, no ask. The point is to start early enough that it is normal by the time it matters.

March to April · freshman year

Secure a freshman summer internship

It does not have to be banking. Wealth management, a local firm, anything finance-adjacent. What matters is having something real on the résumé before sophomore recruiting opens.

Spring to summer · freshman year

Early insight programs

Insight days, spring weeks and early-identification programs. For a non-target-path candidate these are the single highest-value entry point, and several convert directly into summer analyst offers.

Labor Day · sophomore year at UT

Join top clubs and network in earnest

New campus, new network. Get into the organizations that matter for your target groups and start conversations from day one rather than once applications open.

November to February

First rounds

HireVues and first-round interviews, often within days of applying.

February to May

Superdays and offers

Back-to-back interviews, technical and behavioral. Offers often come same-day with short acceptance windows.

Mock benchmarks

Where your rep count should be.

Cumulative totals, not per-stage. These are the numbers the program is built around.

5

Before applications open

Enough to know how a mock runs, where you break under pressure, and what your baseline actually is. Nobody is polished at five.

25

Before first rounds

The real jump. Twenty mocks between applications opening and November is a demanding pace, and it is the stretch where members either commit or fall off. Book them early rather than cramming.

50

Before superdays

By this point the answers should be automatic, so the only thing left to manage is nerves and stamina across back-to-back rounds.

On the pace: fifty mocks across roughly eighteen months is about one a fortnight on average, but the distribution is not even. The heaviest stretch sits between September and November of sophomore year. Plan the 12-hour semester loads with that in mind.
